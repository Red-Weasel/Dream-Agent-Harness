# Research

1. State the question, relevant date and decision criteria. Use supplied material first when it answers the question. Separate facts needed from preferences the user must choose.
2. Use `web_search` to locate sources, then `browse` the relevant original pages. A title or search snippet is a lead, not proof. Prefer official documentation, original research and direct records; check dates and versions.
3. Keep a short evidence table: claim, source URL, date, supporting passage and uncertainty. Compare conflicting evidence before choosing a conclusion. Use `note` for working findings during longer tasks.
4. Answer the actual question, put source links beside supported claims and distinguish observations from inference. Do not invent quotes, citations, access or certainty. If a page is unavailable, name the specific gap and use another suitable source.

For research saved to a file, use `write_file`, check its contents with `read_file`, then return the path. Search or browsing does not authorize sending data, registering accounts or paid requests.

Read [worked examples](references/examples.md) with `skill_file` when a concrete tool sequence would help.
