# Drawing and revising artwork through UI tools

Use for a requested drawing or visual revision in a paint application. Preserve
the chosen application, existing artwork and technique. GUI-only constrains how
you operate the application; it does not prohibit opening or importing the user's
existing work through its UI. Respect separate brief restrictions on reference
imports, tracing, generated art or code-assisted drawing when those are specified.
These are candidate craft procedures informed by artifact critique, alongside
reproduced tool lessons. Better model artwork from this guidance is unmeasured.

## Make the controls predictable

Inspect supplied reference pixels and the current canvas at their actual dimensions.
For polish without a reference, use the saved artwork and requested changes as the
comparison; preserve its style and do not invent a likeness target.
Map the visible canvas origin, zoom and scroll to screenshot coordinates. Screen
coordinates are not artwork coordinates. Recheck the mapping after a layout change.
Verify the active brush, size and foreground color before committing a major pass.

For an unfamiliar brush, make a small reversible mark and inspect the result before
using it on a face or other focal area. Undo and confirm the artwork restored.
Airbrush deposition can depend on time and movement samples: an instantaneous
two-endpoint path may produce only isolated dots. Use timing or intermediate-point
controls only when the actual schema supports them. Inspect the resulting coverage;
more points or longer holds do not necessarily create soft blending. JS Paint's
tested airbrush placed opaque dots, even when more densely sampled.

A cursor or brush-size preview can remain visible after undo. Move the pointer
away, reobserve, or inspect a saved export to distinguish an overlay from painted
pixels. A screenshot of the app and an export of its canvas answer different checks.

## Establish likeness before decoration

At fit-to-view, compare the subject's silhouette, head tilt, shoulder line and
relative head/body size. Use a few reference landmarks relative to the subject's
bounding box; do not transplant coordinates from a previous image. For a portrait,
compare eye line and spacing, nose base, mouth, jaw width and hair mass before
adding lashes, jewelry or strands. Correct the largest discrepancy first.

Block broad light, middle and dark regions to establish form. Judge their size and
placement against the reference, not just whether the chosen skin or clothing
color seems plausible. Heavy uniform outlines and oversized symbolic eyes can
change likeness; use them when the requested style calls for them. Zoom out after
detail work to check that the face still reads at the intended viewing size.

Treat crossed arms and hands as overlapping volumes. Identify which wrist passes
in front, where each palm connects and which fingers are visible. Check silhouette,
negative spaces and connection to the sleeve before drawing nails or accessories.
Hair follows large directional masses and light/shadow groups; many separate lines
do not compensate for a wrong outer shape or flat values.

## Revise the saved work

Save a recoverable checkpoint before changes that are difficult to undo. Compare
the current export with the reference at similar subject scale. Name one visible
defect, make a local correction, and inspect the same region again. Retain a change
because it improves the observed result, not merely because another pass was made.

Finish by inspecting the saved artwork for accidental marks, cut-off anatomy,
unresolved proportions and requested detail. Report what remains approximate. An
export succeeding or a model deciding to stop does not establish faithful likeness.
