# Discovery call preparation

Use this before a first or second conversation with a prospect, when the aim is to understand their situation well
enough to decide whether and how to help.

## Method

1. Collect what is known: the account brief, earlier emails and notes (`library_search`, `recall`, `read_file`).
   List who will attend and their roles.
2. Write the call's goal as a decision: at the end, what should both sides know? Typical: the problem and its cost,
   who decides, how they decide, timing, and whether a next step makes sense.
3. List hypotheses from the research, each with a question that would confirm or kill it.
4. Order the questions: open with their priorities, then the current process and its pain, then impact (time, money,
   risk), then decision process and timing, then what they have tried. Keep them open-ended; prepare one follow-up
   per key question.
5. Prepare short, relevant proof (one example of a similar customer) and decide what not to pitch yet.
6. Draft a 30-minute agenda to share at the start, and a note template for capturing answers.

## Output

A prep sheet saved with `write_file`: goal, attendees, agenda with times, hypotheses and their test questions, the
ordered question list, one proof point, risks (topics that may derail), and the desired next step.

## Checks

- Questions outnumber statements; none can be answered with yes or no if a fuller answer is needed.
- Every hypothesis has a question that could prove it wrong.
- The next step is specific (who, what, when).

## Pitfalls

- Turning discovery into a demo.
- Asking what the website already answers.
- Leaving budget and decision process to "later" and losing weeks.

Limits: preparation guides the conversation; listen for what the plan did not expect and update it afterwards.
